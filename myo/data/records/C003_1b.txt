A 19-year-old previously healthy male was admitted following several days of persistent emesis
 and epigastric discomfort. On presentation, he was hypotensive (80/47 mmHg) and tachycardic 
(124/min) with oxygen saturation 93% . CK-MB at admission was 13.7 U/L; and troponin was 
2.15 ng/ml. EKG showed sinus tachycardia without ischemic changes. 
chest CT demonstrated bilateral parenchymal opacities compatible with pulmonary 
edema. due to Due to hemodynamic instability, he was managed in the ICU with vasopressor
and inotropic support. Echocardiography revealed severely reduced left ventricular systolic 
output with diffuse hypokinesis and pericardial effusion. CXR 
findings were not suggestive of pulmonary congestion. SARS-CoV-2 testing was negative.
 Subsequent coronary angiography disclosed 20–30% LAD narrowing without flow-limiting stenosis.
 Serologic testing indicated recent Coxsackievirus B infection. Cardiac MRI demonstrated 
subepicardial enhancement in the inferolateral segments, consistent with inflammatory 
cardiomyopathy. after initiation of β-blocker and ACE inhibition, ventricular performance 
improved to normal levels and the patient was discharged asymptomatic.
