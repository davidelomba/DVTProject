A 19-year-old man with no prior medical conditions presented with five days of non-bloody, 
 non-bilious vomiting and progressively worsening abdominal pain. On arrival, 
his blood pressure was 80/47 mmHg, heart rate 124/min, temperature 98.2°F, 
and oxygen saturation 93% on room air. EKG showed no ST/T abnormality. Serum cardiac biomarkers were markedly elevated, 
with CK-MB 13.7 U/L (reference ≤10 U/L) and troponin 2.15 ng/ml (reference <0.012 ng/ml). 
Computed tomography (CT) of the abdomen was unremarkable, while chest CT 
demonstrated bilateral pulmonary edema.
He was transferred to the intensive care unit with suspected cardiogenic shock and 
treated with norepinephrine and dobutamine. Transthoracic echocardiography showed 
a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of 20–24%, severe global hypokinesis,
 and mild pericardial effusion. Chest X-ray revealed no pulmonary congestion, 
pleural effusion, or cardiomegaly. SARS-CoV-2 RT-PCR was negative.
On day 3, left heart catheterization showed 20–30% proximal and mid-LAD
 stenosis without significant obstruction. Coxsackievirus B IgG and IgM were positive.
 Cardiac MRI showed typical findings of acute myocarditis with subepicardial and 
mid-myocardial late gadolinium enhancement in the inferolateral walls. 
The patient was started on carvedilol and lisinopril, and repeat echocardiogram
 showed normalization of cardiac output. He recovered fully and was discharged in stable condition.
