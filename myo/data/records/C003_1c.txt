A 19-year-old previously healthy male was admitted following several days of persistent emesis
 and epigastric discomfort. On presentation, he was hypotensive (80/47 mmHg) and tachycardic 
(124/min) with oxygen saturation 93% .CK-MB at admission was 13.7 U/L; and troponin was 
2.15 ng/ml.  EKG showed sinus tachycardia 
Non-contrast abdominal CT was unremarkable. chest CT demonstrated patchy interstitial opacities. 
TTE revealed severe biventricular systolic depression with diffuse wall motion impairment .
 CXR, however, did not show cardiomegaly or pulmonary congestion. 
SARS-CoV-2 PCR was negative.The patient was transferred to the ICU for management of 
heart failure and commenced on norepinephrine and low-dose dobutamine . 
 Invasive coronary evaluation later demonstrated non-occlusive luminal irregularity.
Virological serology demonstrated Coxsackie B serotypes Igm and IgG, compatible with recent infection. 
CMR exhibited patchy subepicardial enhancement in the inferolateral wall with corresponding T1/T2 
prolongation and increased ECV.  Guideline-directed therapy with β-blockade and ACE inhibition was
 initiated once hemodynamics stabilized. Repeat echo before discharge showed normalization of 
cardiac indices and resolution of global hypokinesis. The patient was discharged asymptomatic with plan for ambulatory follow-up.
