A 31-year-old woman with no relevant medical history came to the hospital 
in May 2021 because of several days of chest discomfort, back pain,
 and shortness of breath that appeared after ordinary physical effort. 
She mentioned having received a COVID-19 vaccination with the Pfizer
 vaccine a little over two weeks before the symptoms started and recalled
 mild flu-like feelings the next day. At presentation, she was hemodynamically
 stable but her pulse was slightly elevated. blood tests showed elevated cardiac enzyme activity.The ECG demonestrated
 ST segment elevation of inferolateral segment. Two-dimensional transthoracic strain 
analysis disclosed borderline systolic impairment and hypokinetic foci in the inferolateral 
region. CMR showed T1 and T2 prolongation, findings interpreted as compatible with 
active inflammatory cardiomyopathy. PCR screening for viral pathogens was negative 
and coronary angiography revealed no CAD. Given the mildly depressed 
ventricular function, she was started on beta-blocker and angiotensin-receptor antagonist 
therapy.  At discharge, she reported only positional chest discomfort without ischemic features. 
Follow-up echocardiography showed normal global systolic performance and normalization of segmental kinetics.
 Revaccination with mRNA vaccine was not advised
